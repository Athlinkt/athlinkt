Three to five days in hospital, PT starting there. Two pounds is the lifting limit. Brace whenever I am up. The one stretch I am genuinely out.

Admin and virtual

Weeks one to five. Team meetings on a screen, film, scouting, recruiting, planning. Physical therapy out of the surgeon's office. No court time at all.

October

On court, signing only

Weeks five to ten. I am there and I am coaching, entirely through ASL. Not demonstrating, catching, bending, twisting, pushing or pulling. Anything physical belongs to my staff. The eight-week mark falls mid to late October, so restrictions start easing inside this window.

November

Season opens past the BLT mark

Weeks ten to fourteen. Bending and twisting already cleared. Longer stretches on my feet. Brace by choice at practice, as a cue and a signal.

December

Fully functional

Week fourteen on. Full practice, full travel, normal signing, thirty pound lifting. Still no demonstrating, catching or impact.

Six months lands late February with a month of the season left. PT at GU continues all the way through the year.

Two things worth spelling out

Jargon

BLT means Bending, Lifting, Twisting

The three movements that stress a healing fusion. When a surgeon says "BLT restrictions," that is all it means. Mine run eight weeks, possibly less with clearance. Protocols vary from six to twelve.

The number

Two pounds, not ten

My surgeon set 2 lb for the first two weeks, stricter than the published sheets. Two pounds is a phone and a water bottle. Please do not hand me anything heavier.

Yes

Coaching in ASL

Signing is the whole job for early and mid October. Play calls, corrections, film review, huddles. All of it happens through my hands and none of it through my back.

No

Anything physical

No demonstrating. No catching. No lifting. No bending to the floor. No twisting at the waist. No pushing or pulling carts, doors or equipment.

Ask

What my staff picks up

Balls off the floor, equipment, carts, anything that needs a body rather than a decision. Assign it in August, not in the moment.

Where these numbers come from

Published post-operative protocols, not guesses. Surgeons differ, sometimes a lot, so treat these as the range rather than the rule.

Lifting

2 lb, then 10, then 30

My surgeon set 2 lb for the first two weeks. Published sheets say five to ten. After that, ten pounds through to twelve weeks, then thirty. The limit covers pushing and pulling too.

Bending and twisting

Eight weeks for me

Published protocols range from six weeks to three months. Mine is eight, possibly earlier with clearance. Until then, move your feet to turn, never your torso.

Driving

Two weeks for me

Published sheets say two to eight. Mine is two, conditional on being completely off narcotic pain medication and able to react in an emergency.

Permanent

A lasting lifting cap

Many surgeons set a permanent limit somewhere between twenty and fifty pounds after a lumbar fusion.

One problem, three places

The neck and the mid back are not separate issues. They are what the slip did on its way up.

Where it started

L5–S1 slipped forward

The bone moved off the one below it and the pelvis tipped to compensate. That deepened the curve in my low back, and everything above had to answer for it.

What it forced

The mid back took the strain next

The body has to keep the head balanced over the pelvis. When the base tilts, the mid back changes shape to compensate, and mine has been injured doing that job for years.

Where it ended up

Disc bulges at C5, C6 and C7

The neck compensates last, holding my head level over a spine that was no longer straight underneath it. The bulges in my neck are the top of a chain that starts at the bottom.

What the surgery does

Stops the driver

Fusing L5–S1 ends the movement that caused all of it. What it cannot do is undo years of compensation already built into the mid back and neck. That is why physical therapy runs a full year rather than until the back stops hurting.

Not being fixed

L4–L5 and the annular tear

The tear in the cushion one level up is not part of the surgery and will not be repaired. Once L5–S1 stops moving, that level carries the share the fused joint used to. What I can and cannot do because of it is an ongoing conversation between me and my surgeon, not a date on this page.

What that means

Some limits are permanent

Two activities on the list above stay amber all year for exactly this reason. They are not waiting to be cleared. They get managed, reviewed, and adjusted over time.

These come from published post-operative protocols for lumbar fusion, assembled into a schedule. They are not my surgeon's orders. His restrictions override everything here, and the single biggest question to settle is whether whether this is a one-level or a two-level fusion, which would shift the later months. The calendar assumes surgery around 24 August; a different date shifts every month label. If the plan turns out to be two levels rather than one, most of this shifts later by several weeks.
