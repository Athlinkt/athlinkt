A plain-language walkthrough

What's actually happening in my back

People keep asking why some things wreck me and others don't. Easier to show than explain. Pick any movement and watch.

The short version

One joint. Bottom of the lower back, where the last movable bone sits on the pelvis.

One

The lock is broken

The bony hook that holds that bone to the one below has a gap in it. Not a fresh break that is knitting back together, an old separation that never joined. Both sides, so there is no lock left at all.

Two

The bone slid forward

Without the lock, it moved forward off the one beneath it. More than half its own width now. Doctors grade this in quarters, and mine sits past the halfway mark, at the edge of the next grade up.

Three

The nerve is pinched

The nerve to my leg exits through a small doorway right there. The slide narrowed it on both sides.

And upward

It did not stay in one place

When the base of a spine tilts, everything above compensates to keep the head level. Years of that injured my mid back and left disc bulges in my neck at C5 through C7. Same original problem, three places.

The part nobody expects

Everyone pictures the bone sliding forward. It also turns.

Why

Facet joints block twist

Those bony hooks aren't just a forward stop. They're the main thing preventing one bone rotating on the next.

So

Both sides broken means both jobs gone

Forward slide and rotation. My joint turns and shifts sideways in ways it was never built to.

Feels like

Twisting hurts differently than bending

Hit Top on the 3D and pick a turning movement. Watch it rotate. That's happening right now, every day.

Why no position fixes it

Watch the numbers across a few movements. A trap shows up.

Curl forward

Opens the nerve, feeds the slide

Sitting or folding forward eases the nerve. The same curl lets the bone slide further.

Arch back

Holds the slide, shuts the nerve

Standing tall or lying flat holds the bone. It also closes the doorway. Lying on my back is brutal right now.

No third option

That trade is the reason for surgery

One direction helps the nerve and hurts the slide. The other reverses it. Nothing splits the difference.

What actually helps

Things I've figured out by trial and error. All of them are in the movement list.

Movement

Slow, and never stuck

Speed is the enemy. So is staying in any one position too long. Changing position beats finding the perfect one.

Support

Something to lean on

Leaning forward onto a rolling cart opens the doorway. A cane wouldn't do this. A rollator with a seat is the real version.

Help

Let people lift

Not pride. Bending forward with weight out front is the fastest way to make the slide worse.

Games

A brace, sometimes

A fitted lumbar brace can limit the twist during games. Situational, not all day, and a conversation for my surgical team.

What the surgery does

Switch the view to after the surgery and run the same movements.

One

The motion stops

Two bones become one. Twist and sideways shift go to zero, because there's no joint left to move.

Two

The doorway reopens

A spacer replaces the worn cushion. Restoring that height lifts the doorway open and takes pressure off the nerve.

Three

The slide is held

Hardware holds the bone where the surgeon sets it while the bones grow together over months.

The trade

Load moves up a level

The joint above already shows early wear. Protecting it becomes the long game.

“But won't you lose movement?”

My surgeon said 5 to 10 percent. Here's what that actually means.

The math

One joint out of many

Hips do most of the work when you bend. The mid back does most of the twisting. My lower back has five joints stacked, and this is one of them.

The key part

It's not good movement being removed

This joint doesn't move well right now. It moves wrong. The twist it has is the twist a healthy joint refuses to make.

The trade

Uncontrolled motion for no motion

Not working motion for stiffness. That's a different deal entirely, and a much better one.

Would notice

Deep forward folds

Touching my toes from a seated fold. Reaching all the way to the floor to tie a shoe. Deep backbends. I already avoid all three.

Barely

Turning to sign

That rotation is mostly mid back and hips. Same for squatting to a huddle and getting off the floor, which are hip movements.

Not at all

Everything else

Walking, carrying, standing, sleeping, driving, coaching. Most people with a one-level fusion can't point to the loss in daily life.
