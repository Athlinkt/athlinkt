The operation

What actually happens in the room

Two surgeons, two approaches, one operation. Here is the plan as it has been explained to me.

Two surgeons

This is not a one-doctor operation. Getting to the front of my spine is its own specialty.

In red

The vascular surgeon

Opens the way in and protects everything in the path. The front of the lower spine sits behind major blood vessels and the bowel, so somebody whose whole job is vessels moves them safely aside and holds them there. They get the spine surgeon ready, then stay for the duration.

In white

The spine surgeon

Does the fusion itself. Clears out what is left of the worn disc, places the cage, then moves to my back for the screws and pulls L5 back into position.

In blue

Everyone else

Anaesthesia at my head, scrub tech on instruments, assistants, monitoring, and a circulating nurse who never scrubs in and keeps the room supplied.

How I'll be positioned, and where the cuts are

On my side

Right side down

Not face down, not on my back. I lie on my right side for the whole operation, which is what makes it a lateral approach.

Tilted

Rolled forward by the retractor system

The table and the retractor system pull me forward so my sacroiliac joint is leaned over. That angle is what opens a line to the front of L5–S1 from the side.

Fixed

Left shoulder up and locked

My left shoulder is triangulated toward my neck and held immobile, so nothing shifts once the corridor is set.

The front

Six centimetres, and no muscle cut.

  1. An angled oblique incision, low on the abdomenRoughly six centimetres, placed on the lower side because of where my sacroiliac joint sits. Angled rather than straight.
  2. Nothing is cut throughNo muscle is cut and no muscle is split. The layers are opened and held apart rather than divided.
  3. A corridor about two fingers wideThat is the whole working space. The vascular surgeon retracts what needs moving and protects it there while the spine surgeon works through the gap.
  4. What is left of the disc comes outThe worn L5–S1 disc is cleared from the front, which is the part no posterior approach can reach as completely.
  5. The cage goes inA titanium cage packed with allograft, meaning donor bone. It restores the height that the collapsed disc lost, which is what lifts the nerve doorway back open, and it gives the two bones a scaffold to grow together through.

The back

Two incisions, four screws, then the reduction.

  1. Two incisions near the sacroiliac jointsTwo to three inches each, one on each side.
  2. Four pedicle screwsBilateral posterior pedicle screws, two on each side, anchoring into L5 and into the sacrum below.
  3. L5 gets pulled backWith the cage in front and the screws behind, the surgeon draws L5 back toward where it belongs. As much as it will safely go, which for a slip past halfway is usually a partial correction rather than a full one.

Closing up

Deep

Permanent sutures under the skin

Front and back both. These stay in for good and are never seen.

Middle

Three layers of absorbable sutures

Dissolve on their own over the following weeks. Nothing to have taken out.

Surface

A Mepilex dressing

Soft silicone foam. It lifts off without pulling at the skin.

What waking up is like

The honest version, without either sugar-coating or drama.

Expect

It will hurt

Three incisions, a corridor held open for hours, and hardware that was not there this morning. The acute pain is real and nobody pretends otherwise.

Also true

Patients tolerate it well

This is the phrase that keeps coming up, and it is worth holding onto. Painful and well tolerated are not contradictions.

Then

Up and walking

Three to five days in hospital, walking the first day, physical therapy starting before I leave.
